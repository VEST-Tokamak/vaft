Overview

This module provides the gyration and guiding-centre drift relations of a charged particle, and a Boris integrator of the full Lorentz-force orbit against which the drift relations can be checked.

boris_orbit

Convention-sensitive source 
boris_orbit(q, m, x0, v0, E_field, B_field, dt, n_steps)

Charged-particle orbit from the Lorentz force, by the Boris scheme.

\[m\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf{v}}{\mathrm{d}t} = q\,(\mathbf{E} + \mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B}), \qquad \frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}}{\mathrm{d}t} = \mathbf{v}\]
NameTypeUnitDescription
qfloatC

Particle charge, signed.

mfloatkg

Particle mass.

x0array_likem

Initial position.

v0array_likem/s

Initial velocity.

E_fieldcallableV/m

E_field(x) returning the electric field at position x.

B_fieldcallableT

B_field(x) returning the magnetic field at position x.

dtfloats

Time step.

n_stepsint-

Number of steps.
Returns
positionsnp.ndarraym

Positions, shape (n_steps + 1, 3), the first being x0.

velocitiesnp.ndarraym/s

velocities[i + 1] is the velocity over the step from positions[i] to positions[i + 1]; velocities[0] is v0. Shape (n_steps + 1, 3).

Convention

Non-relativistic leapfrog: velocities live at the half steps between positions (half electric kick, magnetic rotation, half kick, then the position advance). v0 is taken as the velocity half a step before x0. Right-handed Cartesian components.

Raises

  • ValueError m, dt or n_steps is not positive, or a vector is not a 3-vector.
Physical interpretation · Numerical notes · References

Physical interpretation

The full orbit the drift formulas average: integrated in a uniform or slowly varying field, its guiding centre moves at exb_drift_velocity, grad_b_drift_velocity and curvature_drift_velocity.

Numerical notes

The magnetic rotation is exact in angle up to the $\tan(\omega_c\Delta t /2)$ phase error, so with $E = 0$ the kinetic energy is conserved to round-off for any step; the scheme is volume-preserving and has no secular energy drift. Keep $|\omega_c|\Delta t \lesssim 0.1$ for an accurate gyrophase. Fixed step, so the output is deterministic.

References

  1. J. P. Boris, Proc. 4th Conf. Numer. Sim. Plasmas, NRL (1970), 3.
  2. C. K. Birdsall and A. B. Langdon, Plasma Physics via Computer Simulation, McGraw-Hill (1985), Sec. 4-4.
  3. H. Qin et al., Phys. Plasmas 20, 084503 (2013).

Frequency mismatch of a trapped orbit with a toroidal-$n$ perturbation at bounce harmonic $\ell$.

\[\Delta\omega_\mathrm{BH} = \ell\,\omega_b - n\,\omega_E - n\,\omega_B\]
NameTypeUnitDescription
omega_bfloat or np.ndarrayrad/s

Bounce frequency of the trapped orbit.

omega_exbfloat or np.ndarrayrad/s

Toroidal $E\times B$ precession frequency.

omega_magneticfloat or np.ndarrayrad/s

Toroidal magnetic (grad-B and curvature) precession frequency.

lint-

Bounce harmonic, any integer including 0.

nint-

Toroidal mode number of the perturbation.
Returns
—float or np.ndarrayrad/s

$\Delta\omega_\mathrm{BH}$; zero at the resonance.

Convention

The temporal resonance of Park, Boozer and Menard: a static perturbation $\propto e^{in\phi}$ seen by an orbit precessing toroidally at $\omega_E + \omega_B$ and bouncing at $\omega_b$. $\ell = 0$ is the precession resonance. It is not the spatial resonance $m = nq$ of a field line; the two are separate conditions.

Raises

  • ValueError l or n is not an integer.
Physical interpretation · References

Physical interpretation

Where it vanishes the orbit sees a stationary phase of the perturbation each bounce, so the change of $P_\phi$ adds up secularly instead of averaging out – the orbit-level origin of non-resonant transport and NTV, which themselves need the kinetic response of the whole distribution.

References

  1. J.-K. Park, A. H. Boozer and J. E. Menard, Phys. Rev. Lett. 102 (2009) 065002.

Canonical toroidal angular momentum of a particle in an axisymmetric field.

\[P_\phi = m R v_\phi + q R A_\phi\]
NameTypeUnitDescription
qfloatC

Signed particle charge.

mfloatkg

Particle mass.

Rfloat or np.ndarraym

Major radius of the particle.

v_phifloat or np.ndarraym/s

Physical toroidal velocity component $\hat{\boldsymbol\phi}\cdot\mathbf v$.

A_phifloat or np.ndarrayT m

Physical toroidal component of the vector potential.
Returns
—float or np.ndarraykg m^2/s

$P_\phi = \partial\mathcal L/\partial\dot\phi$.

Convention

Cylindrical $(R, \phi, Z)$ right-handed, $\phi$ counter-clockwise seen from above (the IMAS $\phi$). v_phi and A_phi are physical components; $RA_\phi$ is the covariant one, which equals the poloidal flux per radian $\psi$ (see guiding_center_toroidal_momentum). The full particle form: it includes the gyration in $v_\phi$.

Raises

  • ValueError m is not positive or R is negative.
Physical interpretation · Assumptions · References

Physical interpretation

The momentum conjugate to $\phi$. Where $\partial\mathcal L/\partial\phi = 0$ (axisymmetry) it is exactly conserved, which ties the particle’s toroidal velocity to its flux-surface position.

Assumptions

The Lagrangian $\mathcal L = \tfrac12 mv^2 + q\mathbf A\cdot\mathbf v - q\Phi$; conservation needs axisymmetry, $\partial\mathcal L/\partial\phi = 0$.

References

  1. R. B. White, The Theory of Toroidally Confined Plasmas, 3rd ed., Imperial College Press (2014), Ch. 3.

Curvature drift velocity of the guiding centre.

\[\mathbf{v}_R = \frac{m v_\parallel^{2}}{qB^{2}}\, \frac{\mathbf{R}_c\times\mathbf{B}}{R_c^{2}}\]
NameTypeUnitDescription

Returns

Drift velocity.

Convention

$\mathbf{R}_c$ points outward, from the centre of curvature to the line, as in Chen. Charge-signed like the grad-B drift. In a vacuum field $\nabla\times\mathbf{B} = 0$ the two drifts add in the same direction.

Assumptions

$\rho \ll R_c$.

References

exb_drift_velocity(E, B)
Show source vaft/formula/particle.py, lines 209–257

E-cross-B drift velocity of the guiding centre.

\[\mathbf{v}_E = \frac{\mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{B}}{B^{2}}\]
NameTypeUnitDescription
Earray_likeV/m

Electric field, last axis the three Cartesian components.

Barray_likeT

Magnetic field, last axis the three Cartesian components.
Returns
—np.ndarraym/s

Drift velocity, same shape as the broadcast inputs.

Convention

Right-handed Cartesian components. The drift is independent of charge and mass, so ions and electrons move together and no current flows.

Raises

  • ValueError An input is not a 3-vector, or B vanishes.
Physical interpretation · Assumptions · References

Physical interpretation

Over each gyration the particle gains energy on one side of its orbit and loses it on the other, so its radius changes around the orbit and the guiding centre steps sideways – perpendicular to both fields.

Assumptions

$E_\perp \ll cB$ (non-relativistic drift); uniform fields over a Larmor radius and slow in time compared with the gyroperiod.

References

  1. F. F. Chen, Introduction to Plasma Physics and Controlled Fusion, 3rd ed., Springer (2016), Sec. 2.2.2.

Canonical toroidal momentum of a guiding centre, flux plus mechanical parts.

\[P_{\phi,\mathrm{gc}} = q\psi + m v_\parallel R\, b_\phi\]
NameTypeUnitDescription
qfloatC

Signed particle charge.

mfloatkg

Particle mass.

v_parfloat or np.ndarraym/s

Signed parallel velocity, positive along $\mathbf B$.

Rfloat or np.ndarraym

Major radius of the guiding centre.

b_phifloat or np.ndarray-

Toroidal component of the unit vector $\mathbf b = \mathbf B/B$.

psi_per_radianfloat or np.ndarrayWb/rad

Poloidal flux per radian at the guiding centre, $\psi = RA_\phi$.
Returns
—float or np.ndarraykg m^2/s

$P_{\phi,\mathrm{gc}}$.

Convention

$\psi$ is per radian and is the covariant $RA_\phi$ with the IMAS $\phi$: its sign follows the plasma current’s direction in $\phi$, largest on the axis for a current along $+\phi$. A stored COCOS flux is converted by psi_per_radian_from_cocos – $-\psi/(2\pi)$ for COCOS 11 (IMAS DD3), $+\psi/(2\pi)$ for COCOS 17; used as stored it gives the flux term the wrong sign or a $2\pi$ error. $b_\phi v_\parallel$ is the toroidal velocity of the guiding centre; $mRb_\phi v_\parallel = mIv_\parallel/B$ with $I = RB_\phi$.

Raises

  • ValueError m is not positive or R is negative.
Physical interpretation · Assumptions · References

Physical interpretation

Conserved in axisymmetry, so a change of $v_\parallel$ along the orbit is paid for by a change of $\psi$: $\Delta\psi = -(m/q)\Delta(v_\parallel Rb_\phi)$. That is the finite orbit width – the global view of what the grad-B and curvature drifts do locally.

Assumptions

Lowest-order guiding-centre theory: the gyration averaged out, drift velocity small against $v_\parallel$ in the mechanical term.

References

  1. R. B. White, The Theory of Toroidally Confined Plasmas, 3rd ed., Imperial College Press (2014), Ch. 3.
  2. J. Wesson, Tokamaks, 4th ed., Oxford University Press (2011), Sec. 3.12.

magnetic_moment(m, v_perp, B)
Show source vaft/formula/particle.py, lines 452–504

Magnetic moment of a gyrating particle, the first adiabatic invariant.

\[\mu = \frac{m v_\perp^2}{2B}\]
NameTypeUnitDescription
mfloatkg

Particle mass.

v_perpfloat or np.ndarraym/s

Speed perpendicular to $\mathbf B$.

Bfloat or np.ndarrayT

Field strength at the guiding centre.
Returns
—float or np.ndarrayJ/T

$\mu$, non-negative.

Convention

Independent of the charge sign; v_perp enters squared, so its sign is irrelevant. With $E = \tfrac12 mv_\parallel^2 + \mu B$ in a static field it gives the mirror relation of parallel_speed_from_mu.

Raises

  • ValueError m or B is not positive.
Physical interpretation · Assumptions · References

Physical interpretation

The gyro-orbit’s magnetic dipole moment, equivalently its gyro-action $J_\perp = (2m/|q|)\mu$ up to a constant: the invariant of the fastest motion, which is why $v_\perp^2$ tracks $B$ along a field line.

Assumptions

Conserved only under the adiabatic ordering: the field changes little over a gyro-period and a Larmor radius. It is defined for any inputs; its conservation is not implied.

References

  1. F. F. Chen, Introduction to Plasma Physics and Controlled Fusion, 3rd ed., Springer (2016), Sec. 2.3.3.

psi_per_radian_from_cocos

Convention-sensitive source 
psi_per_radian_from_cocos(psi, cocos)
Show source vaft/formula/particle.py, lines 636–687

The guiding-centre $\psi = RA_\phi$ from a flux stored in a COCOS convention.

\[\psi_{RA_\phi} = -\,\sigma_{Bp}\,\sigma_{R\phi Z}\,\frac{\psi_\mathrm{COCOS}}{(2\pi)^{e_{Bp}}}\]
NameTypeUnitDescription
psifloat or np.ndarrayWb or Wb/rad

Poloidal flux as the COCOS convention stores it.

cocosint-

The COCOS index, 1-8 or 11-18.
Returns
—float or np.ndarrayWb/rad

$RA_\phi$ with the IMAS $\phi$, the flux per radian that guiding_center_toroidal_momentum takes.

Convention

$\mathbf B_\mathrm{pol} = \nabla(RA_\phi)\times\nabla\phi$ with $\phi$ counter-clockwise from above, so $RA_\phi$ is largest on the axis for a current along $+\phi$. Sauter’s COCOS flux has $d\psi/d\rho$ of sign $\sigma_{Ip}\sigma_{Bp}$ and its own $\phi$ orientation $\sigma_{R\phi Z}$, hence the factor: $-1/(2\pi)$ for COCOS 11 (IMAS DD3), $+1/(2\pi)$ for COCOS 17 (DD4), $-1$ for COCOS 1. $e_{Bp} = 1$ for 11-18 (whole-turn flux in Wb).

Raises

  • ValueError cocos is not one of the sixteen indices.
Physical interpretation · References

Physical interpretation

Only this signed, per-radian flux makes $q\psi + mv_\parallel Rb_\phi$ a conserved quantity; a COCOS flux used as stored gives the flux term the wrong sign or a $2\pi$ error.

References

  1. O. Sauter and S. Yu. Medvedev, Comput. Phys. Commun. 184 (2013) 293, Eq. 8 and Table I.
