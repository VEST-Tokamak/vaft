Overview

The reductions plasma theory uses between a real torus and an analytic model, kept apart along two independent axes: the geometry (slab, cylindrical, toroidal) and the ordering (local versus global; large aspect ratio $\epsilon = a/R_0 \ll 1$). A slab can carry $q$, $\hat s$, $R_0$ or a $1/R_0$ curvature inherited from a torus and still be a slab; large aspect ratio is an ordering, not a geometry. The guide page Geometric approximations sets out what each representation retains and neglects.

Perturbations are $e^{i(m\theta - n\phi)}$ as in vaft.formula.stability.helical_phase: both mode numbers positive, the helicity in the minus sign; $q$ is a magnitude and $\hat s$ keeps the sign of $d|q|/dr$, so reversed shear flips the sign of $L_s$. A sheared slab is $\mathbf B = B_0(\hat{\mathbf z} + (x/L_s)\hat{\mathbf y})$ with $z$ along the field at $x = 0$. The local slab of a cylinder at $r_0$ is that field-aligned frame: $z$ along $\mathbf B(r_0)$, $y$ the binormal in the surface, $x = r - r_0$. There the harmonic has $k_y = m/r_0$ and $k_z = (m - nq_0)/(q_0R_0)$, zero on a rational surface; along the straightened torus ($z = R_0\phi$) it would be $-n/R_0$ instead. Positive shear makes $L_s = -q_0R_0/\hat s$ negative in that frame.

cylindrical_parallel_wavenumber

Convention-sensitive source 
cylindrical_parallel_wavenumber(m_pol, n_tor, q, R0)

Parallel wavenumber of an $m/n$ harmonic on a cylindrical surface of safety factor $q$.

\[k_\parallel = \frac{m - nq}{qR_0}\]
NameTypeUnitDescription
m_polint-

Poloidal mode number.

n_torint-

Toroidal mode number.

qfloat or np.ndarray-

Safety factor of the surface.

R0floatm

Major radius the cylinder is straightened at.
Returns
—float or np.ndarray1/m

$k_\parallel$; zero exactly where $q = m/n$.

Convention

The harmonic is $e^{i(m\theta - n\phi)}$ (helical_phase) with $z = R_0\phi$, and $B \simeq B_z$ ($B_\theta \ll B_z$). $q$ is the positive magnitude, as the mode numbers are: a negative $q$ from a COCOS sign never reaches $m/n$. With $q$ rising outward, $k_\parallel$ falls through zero at the rational surface.

Raises

  • ValueError A mode number is not positive, or q is zero.
Physical interpretation · Assumptions · References

Physical interpretation

The resonance condition in wave-number form: the perturbation is constant along field lines where $q(r_s) = m/n$, and a rational surface is the place $k_\parallel = 0$.

Assumptions

Cylinder, large aspect ratio: $B_\theta/B_z = r/(qR_0) \ll 1$.

References

  1. J. Wesson, Tokamaks, 4th ed., Oxford University Press (2011), Sec. 6.4.

Safety factor of a straight cylinder standing in for a torus.

NameTypeUnitDescription
rfloat or np.ndarraym

Minor radius.

B_thetafloat or np.ndarrayT

Poloidal (azimuthal) field at $r$.

B_zfloat or np.ndarrayT

Axial field, the stand-in for $B_\phi$.

R0floatm

Major radius the torus is straightened at; the cylinder has length $2\pi R_0$.
Returns
—float or np.ndarray-

$q(r)$.

Convention

$z = R_0\phi$ and $B_z \leftrightarrow B_\phi$: the field-line pitch $d\phi/d\theta$ of the torus becomes $dz/(R_0 d\theta)$. Signs follow the fields; take magnitudes for the usual positive $q$.

Raises

  • ValueError B_theta is zero somewhere.
Physical interpretation · Assumptions · References

Physical interpretation

Toroidal transits per poloidal transit. It is the leading $O(1)$ limit of the toroidal $q$ under $\epsilon = a/R_0 \ll 1$: no $1/R$ variation, no shaping, no Shafranov shift.

Assumptions

A periodic cylinder; a screw pinch has the same $q$ with $R_0$ set by the imposed period.

References

  1. J. Wesson, Tokamaks, 4th ed., Oxford University Press (2011), Sec. 3.3.
  2. J. P. Freidberg, Ideal MHD, Cambridge University Press (2014), Ch. 11 (screw pinch).

local_slab_from_cylinder

Convention-sensitive source 
local_slab_from_cylinder(m_pol, n_tor, r_0, R0, q_0, s_hat)

The field-aligned sheared slab of a cylinder at $r_0$: $(k_y, L_s, k_z)$ of an $m/n$ harmonic.

\[k_y = \frac{m}{r_0},\qquad L_s = -\frac{q_0 R_0}{\hat s},\qquad k_z = \frac{m - nq_0}{q_0R_0}\]
NameTypeUnitDescription
m_polint-

Poloidal mode number.

n_torint-

Toroidal mode number.

r_0floatm

Radius of the reference surface.

R0floatm

Major radius.

q_0float-

Safety factor at $r_0$, a positive magnitude.

s_hatfloat-

Magnetic shear at $r_0$.
Returns
k_yfloat1/m

Binormal wavenumber $m/r_0$.

L_sfloatm

Signed shear length of the local slab.

k_zfloat1/m

Wavenumber along the field at $r_0$; zero when $q_0 = m/n$.

Convention

$x = r - r_0$; $z$ along $\mathbf B(r_0)$ and $y$ the binormal in the surface, the frame of sheared_slab_field, so sheared_slab_parallel_wavenumber(x, *local_slab_from_cylinder(...)) is the cylinder’s $k_\parallel$ to first order in $x$. The harmonic is $e^{i(m\theta - n\phi)}$; its wavenumber along the straightened torus ($z = R_0\phi$) would be $-n/R_0$, which is not this $k_z$. With $\hat s > 0$ the field line, followed in $z$, drifts to $-y$ faster on the outer surfaces, which makes $L_s$ negative in this frame.

Raises

  • ValueError A mode number is not positive, r_0, R0 or q_0 is not positive, or s_hat is zero.
Physical interpretation · Assumptions · References

Physical interpretation

The bridge from global mode numbers to local wavenumbers: on a rational surface $q_0 = m/n$ gives $k_z = 0$, and the shear becomes $k_\parallel’ = k_y/L_s = -k_y\hat s/(q_0R_0)$.

Assumptions

Locality $|x| \ll r_0$ with $q \simeq q_0 + q_0’x$; large aspect ratio ($B_\theta \ll B_z$). The same slab follows from a toroidal equilibrium by a local expansion, without the cylinder in between.

References

  1. H. P. Furth, J. Killeen and M. N. Rosenbluth, Phys. Fluids 6 (1963) 459.
  2. J. Wesson, Tokamaks, 4th ed., Oxford University Press (2011), Sec. 6.8.

shear_length_from_q_R0_s

Convention-sensitive source 
shear_length_from_q_R0_s(q, R0, s_hat)

Magnitude of the shear length a torus or cylinder gives its local slab.

\[L_s = \frac{q R_0}{|\hat s|}\]
NameTypeUnitDescription
qfloat or np.ndarray-

Safety factor at the reference surface.

R0floatm

Major radius.

s_hatfloat or np.ndarray-

Magnetic shear $\hat s = (r/q)\,dq/dr$, for example shear_from_r_q.
Returns
—float or np.ndarraym
$ L_s $, the distance across the surfaces at which the field tilts by $B_y/B_z = 1$ in the local slab.

Convention

Unsigned: magnitudes of $q$ and $\hat s$, whatever sign a COCOS gives them. The sign a slab needs depends on how its $y$ is oriented, see local_slab_from_cylinder for VAFT’s.

Raises

  • ValueError s_hat is zero.
Physical interpretation · Assumptions · References

Physical interpretation

Short $L_s$ means strong shear: a mode stays field-aligned over a thinner layer. Local turbulence and drift-wave models keep this tokamak coefficient while discarding the rest of the geometry.

Assumptions

Large aspect ratio and a locality $ x \ll r$ around the surface.

References

  1. W. Horton, Rev. Mod. Phys. 71 (1999) 735, Sec. II.

sheared_slab_field(x, B0, L_s)

Magnetic field of the sheared slab at radial position $x$.

\[\mathbf B = B_0\left(\hat{\mathbf z} + \frac{x}{L_s}\,\hat{\mathbf y}\right)\]
NameTypeUnitDescription
xfloat or np.ndarraym

Distance from the reference surface.

B0floatT

Field along $z$, the field direction at $x = 0$.

L_sfloatm

Signed shear length.
Returns
—np.ndarrayT

$(B_x, B_y, B_z)$, last axis of length 3.

Convention

$x$ radial, $y$ binormal, $z$ along the field at $x = 0$. The sign of L_s is the sign of the field-line tilt $dB_y/dx$; the slab reached from a positive-shear cylinder by local_slab_from_cylinder has $L_s < 0$.

Raises

  • ValueError L_s is zero or not finite.
Physical interpretation · Assumptions · References

Physical interpretation

The field direction rotates with $x$ while its magnitude stays $B_0$ to first order: magnetic shear without curvature. It is the local model of the region around a rational surface.

Assumptions

$|x| \ll |L_s|$, so $|\mathbf B| \simeq B_0$; no curvature, no equilibrium gradient in the field strength.

References

  1. J. Wesson, Tokamaks, 4th ed., Oxford University Press (2011), Sec. 8.3.

Parallel wavenumber across a sheared slab, to first order in $x/L_s$.

\[k_\parallel(x) = k_z + k_y\,\frac{x}{L_s}\]
NameTypeUnitDescription
xfloat or np.ndarraym

Distance from the reference surface.

k_yfloat1/m

Binormal wavenumber.

L_sfloatm

Signed shear length, as in sheared_slab_field.

k_zfloat1/m

Wavenumber along the field at $x = 0$; zero when $x = 0$ is the resonant surface.
Returns
—float or np.ndarray1/m

$k_\parallel$.

Convention

The field of sheared_slab_field; slab_parallel_wavenumber of that field to first order. With $k_z = 0$ the resonance $k_\parallel = 0$ is at $x = 0$ and $k_\parallel’ = k_y/L_s$.

Raises

  • ValueError L_s is zero or not finite.
Physical interpretation · Assumptions · References

Physical interpretation

A perturbation is field-aligned only on one surface; away from it, $|k_\parallel|$ grows linearly, which is what localises tearing layers, drift waves and ballooning-type modes around rational surfaces.

Assumptions

$ x \ll L_s $, dropping the $O(x^2/L_s^2)$ change of $ \mathbf B $.

References

  1. J. Wesson, Tokamaks, 4th ed., Oxford University Press (2011), Sec. 8.3.

Component of a wavevector along the magnetic field.

\[k_\parallel = \frac{\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf B}{|\mathbf B|}\]
NameTypeUnitDescription
karray_like1/m

Wavevector $(k_x, k_y, k_z)$, last axis of length 3.

Barray_likeT

Magnetic field at the same point, last axis of length 3.
Returns
—float or np.ndarray1/m

Parallel wavenumber, signed along $\mathbf B$.

Convention

Signed: positive when $\mathbf k$ has a component along $\mathbf B$. In a straight slab $\mathbf B = B_0\hat{\mathbf z}$ it is $k_z$; neither curvature nor shear enters unless the field carries them.

Raises

  • ValueError
    A vector is not 3-dimensional or $ \mathbf B = 0$.
Physical interpretation · References

Physical interpretation

How fast a perturbation varies along field lines. Electrons stream along $\mathbf B$, so modes with $k_\parallel \to 0$ – flute-like, field-aligned – are the ones that escape parallel damping.

References

  1. F. F. Chen, Introduction to Plasma Physics and Controlled Fusion, 3rd ed., Springer (2016), Ch. 4.
