Overview

Definitions only (issue #1112). A diffusivity here is either the definition of one – from a mean-square displacement or a velocity autocorrelation – or a reference scale against which a measured or simulated transport coefficient is compared; none is a prediction of the anomalous transport of a plasma. Constitutive fluxes go forward, from coefficients to a flux: a flux alone does not determine a unique pair $(D, V)$, and nothing here inverts one. The complete Braginskii heat diffusivities are vaft.formula.kinetic.classical_electron_heat_diffusivity_from_T_e_B and its ion counterpart; the gyroradius, sound speed and $\rho_*$ have their own owners in vaft.formula.ordering, vaft.formula.sol and vaft.formula.equilibrium (rho_star_from_M_T_B_R_epsilon), and are inputs here.

\[\Gamma = -D\,\partial_r n + nV, \qquad q = -n\chi\,\partial_r T\]

$r$ is the radial coordinate the gradient is taken along, in metres, and an outward flux is positive; a positive $V$ is outward convection, so an inward pinch has $V < 0$. Converting a gradient in a flux label ($\rho$, $\psi_N$) to metres is the caller’s job (#551 / #563). Temperatures are in eV; a flux carries the factor $e$ that turns eV into joules.

Bohm diffusivity: the reference scale of strongly anomalous transport.

\[D_B = C_B\,\frac{T}{|q|B}\]
NameTypeUnitDescription
Tfloat or array-likeeV

Temperature, positive.

Bfloat or array-likeT

Field strength, positive.

Zfloat or array-like-
Charge number of the species, $ q = Ze$, positive.
coefficientfloat-

$C_B$, positive; Bohm’s value $1/16$ by default.
Returns
—float or np.ndarraym^2/s

$D_B$.

Convention

With $T$ in eV and $|q| = Ze$, $T/(|q|B) = T_\mathrm{eV}/(ZB)$ in m^2/s. The coefficient is explicit: $C_B = 1/16$ is Bohm’s empirical value, and comparisons that use $C_B = 1$ must pass it.

Raises

  • ValueError A non-positive or non-finite input.
Validity · References

Validity

Empirical fit. Bohm’s 1949 arc-discharge observation, kept as a reference scale: $D_B \propto T/B$ has no gyroradius dependence, which is what separates Bohm from gyro-Bohm scaling.

References

  1. D. Bohm, in The Characteristics of Electrical Discharges in Magnetic Fields, ed. A. Guthrie and R. K. Wakerling, McGraw-Hill (1949), Ch. 2.

Classical random-walk reference diffusivity: one gyroradius per collision.

\[D_\mathrm{cl} \sim \nu\rho^2\]
NameTypeUnitDescription
nufloat or array-like1/s

Collision frequency that decorrelates the gyro-orbit, non-negative.

rhofloat or array-likem

Gyroradius, the random-walk step, positive.
Returns
—float or np.ndarraym^2/s

$\nu\rho^2$.

Convention

The reference scale without an order-unity prefactor. Which collision frequency and which species’ gyroradius enter is the caller’s choice and sets the meaning: electron collisions with the electron gyroradius give the classical particle diffusivity, ion–ion collisions with the ion gyroradius the classical ion heat diffusivity scale.

Raises

  • ValueError Non-finite input, a negative frequency or a non-positive gyroradius.
Physical interpretation · Limitations · References

Physical interpretation

The random walk of diffusivity_from_mean_square_displacement with step $\rho$ and step time $1/\nu$.

Limitations

An order-of-magnitude scale, not a classical transport model: the full Braginskii coefficients, with their numerical factors and cross terms, are vaft.formula.kinetic.classical_electron_heat_diffusivity_from_T_e_B and vaft.formula.kinetic.classical_ion_heat_diffusivity_from_T_i_B.

References

  1. J. Wesson, Tokamaks, 4th ed., Oxford University Press (2011), Ch. 4.

diffusivity_from_mean_square_displacement

Convention-sensitive source 
diffusivity_from_mean_square_displacement(msd_rate, dimensions=1)
Show source vaft/formula/transport.py, lines 103–152

Diffusivity from the growth rate of the mean-square displacement (Einstein relation).

\[D = \frac{1}{2d}\,\frac{d\langle(\Delta r)^2\rangle}{dt}\]
NameTypeUnitDescription
msd_ratefloat or array-likem^2/s

Rate of growth of the mean-square displacement $d\langle(\Delta r)^2\rangle/dt$, non-negative.

dimensionsint-

Number $d$ of independent directions the displacement is summed over: 1 for a radial displacement alone.
Returns
—float or np.ndarraym^2/s

$D$.

Convention

$(\Delta r)^2$ is summed over the $d$ directions, so a radial-only displacement takes $d = 1$; the factor $1/(2d)$ is explicit, never hidden in the rate. The rate is the long-time (diffusive) slope; estimating it from a trajectory ensemble is a procedure, not part of this definition.

Raises

  • ValueError A non-finite or negative rate, or dimensions not a positive integer.
Physical interpretation · Validity · References

Physical interpretation

For a random walk of step $\Delta$ every $\tau$, $\langle(\Delta r)^2\rangle = (\Delta^2/\tau)\,t$ in one dimension, so $D = \Delta^2/(2\tau)$: the reference behind every $D \sim (\text{step})^2/(\text{time})$ estimate.

Validity

Only where the mean-square displacement grows linearly in time. Ballistic ($\propto t^2$) or sub-/super-diffusive growth has no single $D$.

References

  1. G. I. Taylor, Proc. London Math. Soc. s2-20 (1922) 196.

diffusivity_from_velocity_autocorrelation

Diffusivity as the time integral of the velocity autocorrelation (Taylor–Green–Kubo).

\[D = \int_0^\infty \langle v_r(0)\,v_r(\tau)\rangle\,d\tau\]
NameTypeUnitDescription
lagarray-likes

Lags $\tau$, starting at 0 and increasing.

autocorrelationarray-likem^2/s^2

Velocity autocorrelation $\langle v_r(0)v_r(\tau)\rangle$ at the lags, not normalised.
Returns
—floatm^2/s

$D$ over the sampled lags.

Convention

One velocity component, so the result is the diffusivity along it with no dimensional factor. The upper limit is the last lag supplied: the integral is truncated there, by the trapezoidal rule.

Raises

  • ValueError Non-finite input, mismatched lengths, fewer than two lags, a first lag that is not zero, or lags that do not increase.
Physical interpretation · Validity · Numerical notes · References

Physical interpretation

For $\langle v_r(0)v_r(\tau)\rangle = \langle v_r^2\rangle e^{-\tau/\tau_c}$, $D = \langle v_r^2\rangle\tau_c$: the velocity variance times the correlation time.

Validity

Stationary, homogeneous fluctuations whose autocorrelation decays well within the sampled lags; a slowly decaying or oscillating tail makes the truncated integral depend on the upper limit.

Numerical notes

vaft.formula.utils.trapz_integral, second-order in the lag spacing.

References

  1. G. I. Taylor, Proc. London Math. Soc. s2-20 (1922) 196.
  2. R. Kubo, J. Phys. Soc. Jpn. 12 (1957) 570.

NameTypeUnitDescription

Ion-sound gyroradius, non-zero; its sign (that of the field) does not matter.

c_sfloat or array-likem/s

Ion sound speed, positive.

L_reffloat or array-likem

Normalising length, positive.
Returns
—float or np.ndarraym^2/s

$\chi_\mathrm{gB}$.

Convention

The value depends on which $\rho_s$, $c_s$ and $L_\mathrm{ref}$ are passed; GACODE uses $c_s = \sqrt{T_e/m_D}$, $\rho_s = c_s/(eB_\mathrm{unit}/m_D)$ and $L_\mathrm{ref} = a$ (see vaft.code.gacode.tglf.inputs.TGLFNormalisation). Raises

  • ValueError Non-finite input, a zero gyroradius, or a non-positive speed or length.

Physical interpretation

$\chi_\mathrm{gB} = (\rho_s/L_\mathrm{ref})\,\rho_s c_s$: transport by eddies of size $\rho_s$ decorrelating at $c_s/L_\mathrm{ref}$, smaller than the Bohm scale $\rho_s c_s$ by $\rho_* = \rho_s/L_\mathrm{ref}$.

References

  1. W. Horton, Rev. Mod. Phys. 71 (1999) 735.

Convention

$r$ increases outward and the gradient is taken along it, in metres. A peaked profile ($\partial_r n < 0$) with $D > 0$ diffuses outward; an inward pinch is $V < 0$.

Raises

Physical interpretation

A forward definition of the flux. The inverse is not unique: one flux at one gradient fixes only $-D\,\partial_r n + nV$, not $D$ and $V$ apart; separating them needs transient or modulated data, which is a procedure and not this formula.
